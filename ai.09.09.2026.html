Sep 6 – Sep 9, 2026

Product & platform launches

Meta
Meta launches Muse, a personal AI agent that acts on your behalf [source]
Meta rolled out Muse in the US on September 8, a subscription AI agent that connects to email, calendar, and payment methods to complete multi-step tasks — sending emails, booking trips, even selling a car — inside a sandboxed, approval-based virtual machine. It ships in a free tier (100M tokens/week) plus Power ($20/mo) and Maximum ($100/mo) paid tiers, restricted to users 18+.
Why it matters: A credible, agentic consumer assistant from Meta raises the bar on what "AI agent" means to end users and prospective D365/Power Platform customers — worth watching as a UX and permissioning reference point for Copilot agent design, even though it's a consumer product.

Mistral
Mistral raises €3B Series D led by Samsung at €21B+ valuation [source]
Mistral AI announced on September 8 the largest equity round ever raised by a European tech company: a €3B Series D led by Samsung Electronics (~€1B), with the EU-backed Scaleup Europe Fund and PSG Equity also participating. The company frames the raise around building "sovereign, open-weight AI."
Why it matters: A well-funded, open-weight European alternative matters for Microsoft Partners serving EU customers with data-residency or sovereignty requirements around Copilot/Azure AI Foundry deployments.

Notable research or safety news

OpenAI / Anthropic
OpenAI claims a Navier-Stokes proof; NYU/Anthropic researchers dispute the credit [source]
OpenAI announced on September 8 that an internal, unreleased model directing ~10,000 agents produced a Lean-verified proof that the Navier-Stokes equations can blow up in finite time, a Clay Millennium Prize problem. The same day, NYU's Tristan Buckmaster published his own Lean-verified result (with Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge) on the related forced-Euler equations, and publicly accused OpenAI staff of pressuring him to drop Alpöge's credit after learning of their unpublished work. Neither claim has been peer-reviewed or certified by the Clay Institute.
Why it matters: Beyond the research milestone, the credit dispute directly involves an Anthropic researcher and is a live reputational story across two priority-tier vendors — useful context if customers ask about AI-driven math/scientific claims or vendor trustworthiness.
Google (DeepMind)
DeepMind releases AlphaGenome Atlas, predictions for 9B possible human DNA variants [source]
On September 8, Google DeepMind published the AlphaGenome Atlas, a free, searchable ~1-petabyte database of precomputed molecular-effect predictions for all three possible substitutions at each of the genome's ~3B positions, plus a new AVI score to help researchers prioritize variants. DeepMind is explicit that the atlas is a research tool, not validated for clinical use.
Why it matters: Illustrates Google's continued lead in applying frontier models to life-sciences research — a useful talking point for healthcare/life-sciences vertical conversations even outside core D365 scope.
