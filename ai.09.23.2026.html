Sep 20 – Sep 23, 2026

Model releases & capability updates

Anthropic
Claude Opus 5.5 launches as the new flagship model [source]
Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on September 22, its first model since CEO Dario Amodei's call to pace the frontier. It performs at roughly the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work while costing 40% less to run than Opus 5, and Anthropic says it is the strongest performer yet on its internal alignment audit, more resistant to prompt injection and less prone to hard-to-reverse actions.
Why it matters: A cheaper, more capable Opus tier is directly relevant to any D365/Power Platform partner building Copilot Studio or custom agent workflows on Claude via Azure AI Foundry or the Anthropic API — lower cost per token improves the economics of agentic coding and enterprise knowledge-work use cases.
xAI
Grok 4.7 ships with longer reasoning at unchanged pricing [source]
xAI released Grok 4.7 on September 21, a 2.1-trillion-parameter update to Grok 4.6 that spends more time double-checking its own answers and adds supplemental SpaceX/Starlink engineering data for physical-systems reasoning. Pricing stays flat at $2/$6 per million input/output tokens, and the model rolled out immediately to the Grok app, Cursor, Grok Build, the xAI API, and GitHub Copilot.
Why it matters: Same-day GitHub Copilot availability makes this a low-friction option for engineering teams already standardized on Copilot tooling to benchmark against Microsoft's default models.
Alibaba (Qwen)
Alibaba previews Qwen 4 family at Apsara Conference [source]
At the Apsara Conference in Hangzhou on September 22, Alibaba's Qwen team previewed four upcoming Qwen 4 tiers (Max, Flash, Plus, 27B) built on a new architecture, with CEO Eddie Wu outlining a roadmap toward 5–10 trillion-parameter Qwen 4.5/Qwen 5 models. No release date, pricing, or weights were given — the team said only that it is training and will ship "very soon."
Why it matters: Signals continued fast iteration from a major open-weight vendor; worth tracking for partners evaluating lower-cost or self-hostable alternatives to Western frontier models.

Notable research or safety news

OpenAI
OpenAI to let outside groups assess model safety earlier in development [source]
OpenAI announced on September 23 that it will allow third-party organizations to conduct technical safety assessments during training, evaluation, and deployment of new models, rather than only after development is complete. The company says it is in talks with METR and Redwood Research as potential evaluation partners and laid out principles for independence, scientific rigor, and security in these reviews.
Why it matters: Earlier independent safety review is a governance signal enterprise customers (including regulated D365 customers) increasingly ask about when evaluating AI vendors for compliance-sensitive deployments.
