Product & platform launches

Anthropic
Anthropic launches Claude for Financial Advisors, with Schwab as first RIA custodian [source]
Anthropic unveiled Claude for Financial Advisors on September 14–15: packaged Claude skills for meeting prep, CRM updates, compliance checks, and portfolio-review workflows, connected to custodians and platforms including Charles Schwab, BlackRock, Vanguard, Addepar, and Envestnet. Charles Schwab Advisor Services is the launch custodian, making it available to its 16,000+ independent RIAs; existing Microsoft 365, Salesforce, and DocuSign integrations carry over.
Why it matters: Another vertical-packaged Claude product bundling skills with third-party connectors — a template worth watching for how Anthropic packages regulated-industry workflows, relevant to partners scoping comparable vertical Copilot/Power Platform solutions for wealth management or other regulated customers.
Google
Google opens Claude Opus 5 access to all engineers through its internal Antigravity platform [source]
Google has begun letting engineers company-wide use Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 via its internal Antigravity development platform, reversing a policy that previously restricted outside coding tools like Claude Code or OpenAI Codex to a handful of teams. Usage is quota-limited and positioned as a complement to Gemini, which stays the default internal model.
Why it matters: A notable crack in “eat your own dog food” policy from a hyperscaler and major Anthropic investor — reinforces the multi-model reality (Copilot Studio already supports Claude models too) that D365/Power Platform partners should plan around rather than assuming single-vendor model lock-in.

Partnerships, funding, acquisitions

OpenAI
OpenAI reportedly in early talks for a funding round at a $1.2 trillion valuation [source]
The Financial Times reported September 15 that OpenAI has held early, investor-initiated discussions about a private funding round that would value the company above $1.2 trillion — a 41% jump from its $852B March 2026 round — ahead of a planned IPO. Whether the round proceeds depends on the timing of that IPO; CEO Sam Altman said days earlier that OpenAI would not go public in 2026.
Why it matters: Underscores how much capital keeps flowing to OpenAI even as it delays going public — useful context when customers ask about vendor stability or roadmap continuity for ChatGPT Enterprise or Azure OpenAI-backed Copilot features.

Notable research or safety news

Microsoft
Nadella opens public consultation on a “Humanist AI” Code of Conduct for Microsoft's MAI models [source]
On September 13, CEO Satya Nadella previewed a Code of Conduct governing Microsoft's in-house MAI models, publishing the full document the next day for a six-week public consultation. The rules require MAI models to accept human interruption, correction, and shutdown, bar them from expanding their own scope or hiding their reasoning, and prohibit uses like cyberattacks, nuclear-weapons assistance, or deepfakes. A revised version is planned for later in 2026 to guide 2027 model development.
Why it matters: Sets Microsoft's public bar for “safe” first-party AI behavior right as Copilot and Dynamics 365/Power Platform agents increasingly run on MAI models alongside OpenAI and Anthropic — worth tracking since it will likely shape the governance and compliance language partners have to answer for in agent deployments.
OpenAI, Anthropic & Google
OpenAI confirms weeks of talks with Anthropic and Google on a shared AI-safety standards body [source]
On September 15, OpenAI said it has been in discussions for several weeks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind about coordinating on AI safety — including shared technical evaluations, pre-release audits, and a FINRA-style self-regulatory body first proposed by DeepMind's Demis Hassabis. OpenAI said it doesn't believe the three companies need an antitrust waiver to coordinate on safety. The talks follow a widely discussed essay from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei urging the industry to slow capability gains.
Why it matters: A joint safety framework across the three vendors most likely to sit behind Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise, and Claude-based tooling could eventually mean shared audit/evaluation standards partners can point to when customers ask about AI risk governance.
