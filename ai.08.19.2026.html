Product & platform launches

OpenAI
OpenAI launches a safety-hardened ChatGPT for teens [source]
OpenAI rolled out a teen-specific ChatGPT experience for 13-17 year-olds on August 18, using age-prediction to automatically route likely-minors into a mode that blocks suicide, self-harm, and romantic/sexual conversations. Homework help is also tuned to guide students toward answers rather than hand them over directly.
Why it matters: Signals OpenAI's response to mounting regulatory and safety scrutiny of minors using AI chatbots; relevant if your org deploys OpenAI-based tools where age-gating or content controls could become a compliance expectation.

Partnerships, funding, acquisitions

Anthropic
Anthropic's revenue run rate tops $65B as a fall IPO looms [source]
Anthropic told investors its annualized revenue run rate hit $65 billion by the end of July, up more than sevenfold year-over-year, with Q2 revenue over $11.5B (versus $787M in Q2 2025) and positive adjusted operating income. Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan are reportedly preparing the company for a potential fall IPO.
Why it matters: A near-term Anthropic IPO would be a major AI-market milestone and underscores fast-scaling enterprise Claude adoption, including via Microsoft Foundry — worth tracking for vendor stability and pricing-power implications.
OpenRouter / Stripe
Stripe agrees to buy AI model router OpenRouter for $7B+ [source]
Stripe finalized a deal to acquire OpenRouter, the gateway roughly 8 million developers use to route across 400+ models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and DeepSeek, for more than $7 billion — a 5.4x markup over its $1.3B valuation just three months earlier. OpenRouter reportedly processed about 1.5 quadrillion tokens over the past year.
Why it matters: Consolidation of multi-vendor AI model routing into a payments giant could reshape how enterprises pay for and switch between AI vendors — relevant background if your stack ever needs multi-model flexibility outside Azure AI Foundry.

Notable research or safety news

OpenAI
OpenAI restructures its frontier-risk "Preparedness" function amid a wider safety overhaul [source]
Multiple reports say OpenAI disbanded its centralized Preparedness team, which evaluated whether frontier models could pose catastrophic risks, and redistributed staff into cyber-, bio-, and self-improvement-focused groups reporting to head of safety Saachi Jain; OpenAI disputes the "disbanded" characterization. The shake-up follows OpenAI's decision to pause its next frontier model, Astra, after internal evaluations showed it crossing a "critical" cybersecurity capability threshold for the first time.
Why it matters: Any partner building on OpenAI's frontier model roadmap should watch for a slower or altered release cadence and evolving safety-review requirements as a result of this restructuring.
